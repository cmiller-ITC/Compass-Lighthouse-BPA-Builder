# Mission

Compass exists to help clinicians think more clearly---not faster.

The purpose of Compass is not to replace clinical judgment.

The purpose is to reduce cognitive load so clinicians can devote more
attention to the person sitting in front of them.

Compass exists to transform complexity into understanding.

Every feature should answer one question:

> "Does this help the clinician better understand this person and know
> what to do next?"

If the answer is no, it does not belong in Compass.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Clinical Containment

One of the primary responsibilities of Compass is helping clinicians
hold complexity without becoming overwhelmed by it.

Clients rarely present with one isolated problem.

They present with histories...

relationships...

trauma...

medical conditions...

financial realities...

developmental transitions...

strengths...

barriers...

responsibilities...

hopes...

and fears---

all interacting simultaneously.

Compass exists to organize complexity into something understandable,
actionable, and compassionate.

The goal is never to oversimplify.

The goal is to create enough clarity that both clinician and client know
where to begin.

Clinical containment also means helping the clinician distinguish:

-   what is important
-   what is urgent
-   what is meaningful but can wait
-   what remains uncertain
-   what is outside the client's current capacity
-   what small action may meaningfully improve life right now

Compass should help clinicians see the forest without losing the trees.

# Core Philosophy

Compass is built around a clinical reasoning spine.

These questions become the thinking framework for every feature inside
the platform.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. What is happening now?

Before explaining the problem, Compass first helps the clinician see the
current picture clearly.

Understand:

-   presenting concerns
-   current symptoms
-   functional impairment
-   immediate context
-   client-defined concerns
-   risk and safety
-   current supports
-   strengths already visible
-   what remains unclear

Observation comes before interpretation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. What happened to this person?

Understand:

-   developmental experiences
-   attachment
-   trauma
-   relationships
-   culture
-   identity
-   environment
-   medical history
-   life context

Never begin with diagnosis.

Always begin with the story.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. How did they survive?

Symptoms are viewed as adaptations before they are viewed as pathology.

Compass identifies:

-   coping patterns
-   nervous system adaptations
-   attachment strategies
-   emotional regulation
-   avoidance
-   beliefs
-   behavioral responses
-   protective strategies
-   survival strategies

The question is not:

"What's wrong with them?"

The question is:

"How did this make sense?"

Adaptation should be understood compassionately without assuming that
every symptom or behavior is trauma-derived.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. What keeps the problem going?

Compass continuously searches for maintaining factors.

Examples include:

-   avoidance
-   reinforcement loops
-   cognitive patterns
-   emotional cycles
-   interpersonal dynamics
-   environmental barriers
-   nervous system activation
-   practical constraints
-   sleep disruption
-   medical contributors
-   ongoing exposure to destabilizing circumstances
-   patterns that provide short-term relief while creating long-term
    cost

This is the engine of conceptualization.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. What matters most right now?

Not every problem deserves equal attention.

Compass helps clinicians determine:

What requires stabilization?

What requires treatment?

What requires practical problem-solving?

What requires protection?

What requires cultivation?

What could meaningfully improve quality of life now?

What can reasonably wait?

Clinical prioritization is one of Compass' primary responsibilities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. What strengths already exist?

Compass actively searches for:

-   resilience
-   insight
-   motivation
-   relationships
-   protective factors
-   existing skills
-   values
-   hope
-   resources
-   prior successes
-   adaptive capacities

Treatment begins with existing capacity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. What is realistically possible?

The ideal intervention is not always the best intervention.

Compass considers:

Clinical need

-   

Current safety

-   

Environmental reality

-   

Available resources

-   

Financial barriers

-   

Transportation

-   

Housing

-   

Caregiving responsibilities

-   

Emotional bandwidth

-   

Readiness for change

-   

Access to treatment

↓

Realistic next steps

A clinically elegant plan that the client cannot use is not an effective
plan.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Where do we begin?

Only after understanding:

What is happening now

↓

What happened

↓

How they survived

↓

What keeps it going

↓

What matters most

↓

What strengths exist

↓

What is realistically possible

↓

does Compass begin generating:

-   diagnostic considerations
-   treatment priorities
-   interventions
-   documentation
-   treatment planning
-   progress indicators

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 9. What have we learned, and what remains uncertain?

Compass should continually return to the whole story.

It should ask:

-   What conclusions are supported?
-   What remains uncertain?
-   What does not yet fit?
-   What changed our understanding?
-   What needs to be revisited?
-   Does the plan still follow logically from the formulation?
-   Does the documentation accurately reflect the person and the
    evidence?

Clinical reasoning remains iterative.

The formulation is allowed to change.

# The Compass Rooms

The Compass Rooms operationalize the clinical reasoning spine.

They are not separate datasets.

They are not decorative categories.

They are not five different versions of the client.

They are **distinct clinical reasoning lenses applied to the same
evolving human story**.

> One evolving clinical story → multiple purposeful clinical lenses →
> one integrated understanding and plan.

Every Room must have a clear clinical job.

Every piece of narrative, data, guidance, coaching, questioning, or
recommendation shown inside a Room must serve that Room's clinical
purpose.

If an element cannot answer the question:

> "Why does the clinician need this in this Room?"

it does not belong there.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌊 Observation Room

### What are we seeing?

Observation helps the clinician stay close to the client's story before
deciding what it means.

It may contain:

-   Live Clinical Overview
-   Story So Far
-   current concerns
-   symptoms
-   functional impact
-   client goals
-   current context
-   emerging themes
-   strengths already visible
-   missing information
-   contradictions or unclear areas
-   "Compass Is Wondering..." prompts
-   natural-language questions to ask next

Observation describes before explaining.

Observation should not prematurely diagnose, formulate causation, or
recommend treatment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌿 Understanding Room

### How might this make sense?

Understanding develops the formulation.

It asks:

-   What happened to them?
-   How did they adapt, cope, protect themselves, or survive?
-   What patterns developed?
-   What biological, developmental, psychological, relational, medical,
    social, environmental, or systemic factors may help explain the
    current picture?
-   What strengths or protective processes developed alongside the
    difficulties?

It may contain:

-   predisposing factors
-   precipitating factors
-   adaptations and survival strategies
-   attachment and relational patterns
-   trauma-related patterns when supported
-   cognitive, emotional, behavioral, and physiological processes
-   protective factors
-   competing hypotheses
-   supporting evidence
-   missing evidence
-   questions that could strengthen or weaken the formulation

Understanding should formulate without treating hypotheses as facts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧭 Navigation Room

### What matters most right now?

Navigation helps the clinician see the forest for the trees.

It asks:

-   What is keeping the problem going?
-   What requires attention first?
-   What could create the greatest meaningful relief or improvement?
-   What can wait?
-   What additional information could materially change the direction?

It may contain:

-   maintaining mechanisms
-   safety and stabilization priorities
-   functional priorities
-   client priorities
-   diagnostic and differential questions that affect direction
-   medical or psychiatric rule-outs
-   readiness
-   capacity
-   environmental realities
-   barriers
-   clinical leverage points
-   sequencing considerations

Navigation should reduce complexity into a manageable direction without
oversimplifying the person.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌱 Growth Room

### What could help, and what is feasible now?

Growth translates understanding and priorities into realistic
opportunities for change.

It asks:

-   What strengths and tools already exist?
-   What could be strengthened?
-   What intervention logically follows from the formulation?
-   What can this person realistically do now?
-   What is the smallest meaningful next step?

It may contain:

-   client-defined goals
-   strengths and protective factors
-   existing coping tools
-   evidence-informed treatment considerations
-   skill-building opportunities
-   psychoeducation
-   behavioral experiments
-   environmental or relational supports
-   treatment sequencing
-   readiness
-   barriers
-   progress indicators

Growth should not become a treatment-menu generator.

The best recommendation is not necessarily the most comprehensive
intervention.

It is the clinically coherent intervention the person can realistically
use.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 📖 Reflection Room

### What have we learned, and how do we carry it forward?

Reflection integrates the clinical picture.

It asks:

-   What is our current formulation?
-   What conclusions are sufficiently supported?
-   What remains uncertain?
-   What needs to be revisited?
-   Does the diagnosis fit the evidence?
-   Does the treatment direction follow from the formulation?
-   Does the documentation preserve the Golden Thread?

It may contain:

-   current formulation
-   diagnostic summary and rationale
-   ICD-10 support
-   evidence for and against diagnostic possibilities
-   remaining uncertainty
-   risk and protective factors
-   Golden Thread review
-   documentation readiness
-   documentation preview
-   medical necessity and level-of-care support when needed
-   items to monitor or reassess

Reflection should never hide uncertainty for the sake of producing a
polished final product.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Room Boundaries

Room boundaries are clinically meaningful.

Compass should not allow every Room to do everything.

**Observation describes before interpreting.**

**Understanding formulates before prioritizing.**

**Navigation prioritizes before planning.**

**Growth translates formulation and priorities into feasible change.**

**Reflection integrates, evaluates, and documents.**

The Rooms should build upon one another without becoming rigid gates.

Clinicians may move between Rooms as new information changes the
clinical picture.

The purpose of sequencing is to prevent premature closure---not to force
linear thinking.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Shared Story, Different Lens

Every Room uses the same underlying assessment evidence.

A Room does not maintain a separate copy of the client.

New information entered during the assessment should update all relevant
clinical reasoning.

The same piece of information may appropriately appear differently
across Rooms because each Room asks a different question of it.

For example:

-   Observation may identify avoidance as a current behavior.
-   Understanding may explore how avoidance developed or what function
    it serves.
-   Navigation may identify avoidance as a maintaining factor or
    priority.
-   Growth may consider a feasible intervention targeting avoidance.
-   Reflection may connect the pattern to formulation, diagnosis,
    treatment direction, and documentation.

Same evidence.

Different clinical function.

One story.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Recovery Philosophy

Recovery is not synonymous with symptom reduction.

Healing may involve:

changing something

leaving something

accepting something

building something

protecting something

creating something

Treatment is successful when clients are moving toward a meaningful
life---not merely away from symptoms.

Compass helps clinicians reduce suffering while simultaneously helping
clients build lives worth living.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Human Bandwidth

Human beings possess finite emotional, cognitive, financial, social, and
physiological bandwidth.

Compass should never create treatment plans that exceed a person's
current capacity.

Instead, Compass seeks the greatest meaningful progress within the
client's present reality.

Treatment planning should feel achievable---not overwhelming.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Minimum Effective Change

When a client's life is highly complex, unstable, unsafe,
resource-limited, or overwhelming, Compass should help identify the
**smallest clinically meaningful change that could improve functioning,
safety, agency, or quality of life**.

The goal is not minimal care.

The goal is **minimum unnecessary burden**.

Sometimes the appropriate next step is comprehensive treatment.

Sometimes it is one boundary.

One coping strategy.

One practical resource.

One safer interaction pattern.

One stabilizing routine.

One conversation.

One moment of increased choice.

Compass should never confuse complexity of presentation with a
requirement for complexity of intervention.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Clinical Feasibility

A recommendation is not clinically useful merely because it is
theoretically appropriate.

Compass should evaluate recommendations through the lens of feasibility.

Clinical feasibility includes:

-   current safety
-   cognitive and emotional bandwidth
-   readiness
-   access
-   cost
-   transportation
-   housing
-   caregiving
-   work demands
-   physical health
-   social support
-   environmental constraints
-   treatment availability
-   client preference

When ideal care is not currently accessible, Compass should help the
clinician identify what can still be done meaningfully within the
client's present reality.

# Information Hierarchy

Compass prioritizes information in this order:

1.  Client narrative
2.  Current reality
3.  Clinical meaning
4.  Clinical priorities
5.  Strengths and capacity
6.  Evidence
7.  Recommendations
8.  Documentation

Documentation should always emerge naturally from understanding.

Never reverse this order.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Documentation Philosophy

Documentation is a byproduct of good clinical thinking.

Clinicians should never manually reconstruct what they have already
assessed.

Compass automatically generates documentation directly from the
completed assessment and formulation.

Documentation should always remain evidence-based.

Required documentation may include:

-   Medical necessity
-   Functional impairment
-   Diagnostic rationale
-   Level of care justification
-   Risk statements
-   Progress summaries
-   Treatment justification
-   Required regulatory components

Many documentation elements should remain hidden unless needed.

The clinician should experience a clean workspace while Compass
maintains the regulatory framework beneath the surface.

The final note should contain what the clinician truly needs to
document---not every piece of reasoning Compass performed internally.

# Clinical Navigation

Compass does not simply organize information.

Compass helps clinicians navigate it.

Throughout the interview Compass continuously asks:

What matters?

What connects?

What is missing?

What deserves attention first?

What questions would improve understanding?

What barriers may interfere with treatment?

What strengths can be leveraged immediately?

What is feasible now?

What can wait?

Clinical navigation should feel like thoughtful supervision---not
automated advice.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Real-Time Clinical Guidance

Compass behaves like a trusted clinical supervisor sitting quietly
beside the clinician.

Guidance should be:

timely

brief

specific

clinically meaningful

non-intrusive

Guidance may include:

-   suggested questions
-   natural-language verbal prompts
-   observations
-   competing hypotheses
-   rapport suggestions
-   psychoeducation
-   clarification prompts
-   diagnostic considerations
-   treatment implications
-   feasibility considerations
-   reminders to slow down or gather more evidence

Clinical guidance should be woven organically throughout the assessment
and the Rooms.

It should not be isolated inside a single "Coach" destination.

The right guidance should appear where the clinician is already
thinking.

The goal is never to interrupt.

The goal is to gently expand clinical thinking.

# Navigation Philosophy

The interface should mirror how clinicians think.

Not how software is organized.

Every screen should naturally answer:

"What do I need right now?"

The clinician should never wonder:

"Where do I put this?"

or

"What should I do next?"

Navigation should feel intuitive because it follows clinical
reasoning---not software architecture.

# Workspace Philosophy

Compass supports two primary experiences:

## Assessment Mode

The clinician is gathering information with the client.

The assessment form and section navigation remain primary.

Compass provides quiet real-time support and access to the Compass
Rooms.

## Room Mode

The clinician has entered a focused clinical reasoning workspace.

Selecting a Room should feel like moving into a destination---not
expanding another card inside a sidebar.

When a Room is open:

-   The selected Room becomes the primary workspace.
-   The ordinary assessment form and left-side assessment navigation may
    temporarily move out of view.
-   The Room should have enough visual and cognitive space to support
    deeper thinking.
-   Only one Room should be open at a time.
-   A clear path back to the assessment and/or Room lobby must always be
    available.
-   The Room remains connected to the live assessment data.
-   The transition should feel calm, intentional, and reversible.

A full-workspace experience is preferred over forcing complex clinical
reasoning into a narrow side panel.

# Visibility Rules

Everything Compass knows does not need to be displayed.

Visible:

-   Executive Summary
-   Clinical Conceptualization
-   Clinical Priorities
-   Current Themes
-   Strengths
-   Missing Information
-   Suggested Questions
-   Treatment Direction
-   Relevant uncertainty
-   Feasible next steps

Hidden but Available:

-   ICD-10 reasoning
-   Diagnostic confidence
-   Medical necessity
-   Level of care justification
-   Billing language
-   Regulatory documentation
-   Quality metrics
-   Confidence calculations
-   Evidence mapping
-   Internal scoring logic

The clinician sees only what improves decision making.

Hidden intelligence should remain available when clinically,
administratively, or regulatory necessary.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Diagnostic Philosophy

Diagnosis should emerge from understanding.

Compass may assist with:

-   diagnostic possibilities
-   differential diagnosis
-   supporting evidence
-   contradictory evidence
-   missing criteria
-   confidence
-   ICD-10 codes

Compass should never treat a code as the clinical understanding.

The diagnosis is one representation of the formulation---not the
formulation itself.

ICD-10 support should reduce clinician burden while preserving clinician
authority.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Safety and Clinical Urgency

The clinical reasoning sequence should never delay attention to
immediate safety or urgent medical concerns.

Safety may interrupt the normal Room flow.

When clinically significant risk information emerges, Compass should
make the concern visible, identify relevant missing information, and
support appropriate clinician assessment.

Safety guidance should be clear without becoming visually alarmist.

The clinician remains responsible for risk determination and action.

# Compass Success Test

Every feature must answer:

Does this merely collect information?

or

Does it help the clinician understand what this information means for
how this particular human being moves forward?

For Room features, also ask:

> "Which clinical question does this help answer, and why does it belong
> in this Room?"

If neither question has a clear answer---

Compass isn't finished thinking yet.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Non-Negotiable Rules

Never diagnose before understanding.

Never recommend before conceptualizing.

Never document something unsupported by evidence.

Never confuse hypothesis with fact.

Never duplicate clinician work.

Never overwhelm a client with a plan they cannot realistically carry.

Never allow documentation polish to outrun clinical understanding.

Never let five Rooms become five disconnected versions of the same
story.

Reduce clicks.

Reduce typing.

Reduce cognitive load.

Increase curiosity.

Increase confidence.

Increase clinical quality.

Preserve clinical autonomy.

Honor uncertainty.

Honor adaptation.

Honor human bandwidth.

Prioritize meaning over efficiency.

Prioritize feasible change over theoretical perfection.

Always keep the patient---not the paperwork---at the center.

# Compass Promise

Compass does not replace clinicians.

Compass does not replace relationships.

Compass does not replace clinical wisdom.

Compass helps clinicians hold complexity.

Compass helps clinicians think more clearly.

Compass helps clinicians understand how a person's story, adaptations,
current context, strengths, and maintaining patterns fit together.

Compass helps clinicians determine what matters most and what is
realistically possible.

Compass transforms complexity into an understandable, prioritized,
compassionate, and feasible path forward.

So clinicians can spend less time managing information and paperwork...

and more time being fully present with the people they serve.
