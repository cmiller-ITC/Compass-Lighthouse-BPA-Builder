# Purpose

Behavioral health assessments require clinicians to gather, organize,
interpret, and integrate an extraordinary amount of information within a
limited period of time. During a typical 50--60 minute intake,
clinicians must simultaneously establish rapport, obtain a comprehensive
history, recognize diagnostic patterns, assess risk, determine
appropriate treatment recommendations, and create documentation that
accurately reflects the client's presentation.

This cognitive burden is substantial and increases the likelihood that
meaningful clinical patterns, inconsistencies, or opportunities for
deeper exploration may be overlooked.

The Compass Intelligence Panel was created to reduce this burden.

Rather than functioning as a documentation assistant or AI chatbot,
Compass serves as a real-time clinical navigation and reasoning system
that continuously helps clinicians organize, interpret, and refine their
understanding of the person sitting in front of them.

Its purpose is not simply to help clinicians complete an assessment.

Its purpose is to help clinicians \*\*\*\*understand the person as
completely, accurately, and compassionately as possible within the time
available\*\*\*\*, allowing them to make the most informed diagnostic
and treatment decisions possible.

Compass functions like an experienced clinical supervisor who actively
thinks alongside the clinician throughout the interview---continuously
observing, synthesizing, and proactively offering guidance while
preserving complete clinician autonomy.

**---**

# Vision

The Compass Intelligence Panel is the clinician's real-time clinical
thinking partner.

It continuously observes the evolving assessment, identifies meaningful
clinical patterns, synthesizes information into an evolving
understanding of the client's story, and proactively offers
evidence-based guidance throughout the interview.

Rather than replacing clinical judgment, Compass exists to strengthen
it.

The clinician remains the decision maker.

Compass simply helps them think more clearly.

**---**

# Clinical Mission

Throughout every assessment, Compass should help the clinician hold the
whole person while moving through a coherent clinical reasoning spine:

1.  **What is happening now?**
2.  **What happened to them?**
3.  **How did they adapt, cope, protect themselves, or survive?**
4.  **What is keeping the current difficulties going?**
5.  **What matters most right now?**
6.  **What change is realistic, meaningful, and clinically
    appropriate?**
7.  **What have we learned, what remains uncertain, and how do we carry
    that understanding forward?**

These questions organize the clinician's movement from observation to
understanding, prioritization, feasible change, and integration.

Compass should also continuously help the clinician ask:

-   What evidence supports what I am seeing?
-   What important information is still missing?
-   What does not yet fit?
-   What should I explore next?
-   How confident should I be in the current interpretation?

Every feature within Compass Intelligence should directly support one or
more of these clinical reasoning tasks.

The framework is an organizing model, not a rigid causal sequence.
Biological, developmental, psychological, relational, social, cultural,
environmental, medical, and systemic contributors should remain visible
whenever supported by the assessment.

# Intelligence Cycle

Compass continuously operates using a five-stage clinical reasoning
loop.

## 1. Observe

Continuously monitor all available assessment information including:

-   Assessment responses
-   Narrative text
-   Symptom selections
-   Standardized measures
-   Mental Status Examination
-   Timeline information
-   Functional impairment
-   Risk factors
-   Psychiatric history
-   Medical history
-   Social history
-   Clinician-entered observations

**---**

## 2. Interpret

Analyze incoming information to identify:

-   Clinical themes
-   Symptom patterns
-   Functional impairments
-   Protective factors
-   Risk indicators
-   Strengths
-   Inconsistencies
-   Missing information
-   Diagnostic possibilities

**---**

## 3. Synthesize

Integrate all available information into an evolving understanding of
the client's story.

Rather than viewing each response independently, Compass should
continuously connect information across multiple assessment domains to
develop a comprehensive clinical formulation.

**---**

## 4. Guide

Provide proactive clinical guidance that helps the clinician think more
effectively.

Examples include:

-   Executive summaries
-   Clinical snapshots
-   Emerging themes
-   Clarifying questions
-   Diagnostic considerations
-   ICD-10 diagnostic suggestions
-   Treatment implications
-   Risk awareness
-   Documentation guidance

**---**

## 5. Update

Immediately reflect meaningful changes as new information becomes
available.

The Intelligence Panel should feel alive, continuously adapting its
understanding of the case as the interview progresses.

# Core Design Principles

## Clinical First

Every recommendation must originate from documented clinical evidence.

Compass should never invent symptoms, fabricate history, or make
unsupported assumptions.

**---**

## Understand Before Diagnosing

The primary objective is understanding---not assigning diagnoses.

Diagnosis is the result of thoughtful clinical reasoning rather than the
starting point.

**---**

## Proactive Guidance

Compass is intentionally proactive.

It should never wait for the clinician to request assistance.

Instead, it continuously evaluates the assessment and independently
offers guidance whenever meaningful clinical changes occur.

Examples include:

-   Newly emerging themes
-   Diagnostic confidence changes
-   Missing information
-   Contradictory evidence
-   Recommended follow-up questions
-   Treatment implications
-   Documentation gaps

Guidance should always feel timely, relevant, and supportive without
interrupting the therapeutic interaction.

**---**

## Transparent Clinical Reasoning

Compass should never function as a black box.

Every recommendation should answer:

-   What am I seeing?
-   Why do I think this?
-   What evidence supports it?
-   What evidence is still missing?
-   How confident am I?

**---**

## Dynamic

Compass is never static.

Every response entered during the assessment has the potential to
strengthen, weaken, refine, or redirect the evolving clinical
understanding.

**---**

## Assist, Never Replace

Compass recommends.

Clinicians decide.

The clinician always maintains authority over diagnostic impressions,
treatment decisions, and documentation.

# Intelligence Outputs

Compass continuously generates clinically meaningful intelligence, but
these outputs should **not** exist as a competing set of permanent tabs.

Instead, intelligence functions should appear contextually inside the
clinical workspace where they are most useful.

Core intelligence functions include:

-   Executive Summary
-   Clinical Snapshot
-   What I'm Seeing
-   What I'm Wondering
-   What Doesn't Yet Add Up
-   Clarifying Questions
-   Clinical Themes
-   Differential Diagnosis
-   Risk Awareness
-   Strengths and Protective Factors
-   Treatment Considerations
-   Documentation Readiness
-   Documentation Preview
-   ICD-10 diagnostic support

The same output may evolve as new evidence is entered, but it should be
presented through the clinical lens appropriate to the clinician's
current task.

## Executive Summary

A real-time bird's-eye view of the case.

This section should answer:

> "If another clinician walked into the room right now, what would they
> need to know within 30 seconds?"

The Executive Summary should remain concise and should not outrun the
available evidence.

## Clinical Snapshot

A concise clinical narrative summarizing the client's presentation,
major concerns, functional impact, relevant context, and the current
level of clinical understanding.

The Clinical Snapshot should evolve as the assessment develops.

## What I'm Seeing

Observed clinical themes and meaningful patterns.

These are observations---not diagnoses.

## What I'm Wondering / What Doesn't Yet Add Up

Potential hypotheses, inconsistencies, unanswered questions, and areas
requiring clarification.

The purpose is curiosity rather than contradiction.

## Clarifying Questions

Thoughtful, timely follow-up questions that deepen understanding, test
hypotheses, reduce uncertainty, and identify missing information.

Questions should be phrased so they can be used naturally in the
interview whenever possible.

## Differential Diagnosis and ICD-10 Support

Compass may surface diagnostic possibilities and corresponding ICD-10
options when sufficient evidence exists.

Each diagnostic consideration should distinguish:

-   Supporting evidence
-   Missing evidence
-   Contradictory or alternative evidence
-   Confidence level
-   Recommended clarification questions

Diagnostic support should remain downstream from understanding.

The clinician remains responsible for diagnosis and code selection.

## Treatment Considerations

Evidence-informed possibilities that logically follow from the emerging
formulation.

Treatment guidance should consider:

-   The client's goals
-   The mechanism or problem being targeted
-   Readiness
-   Current capacity and emotional bandwidth
-   Safety
-   Access and resources
-   Environmental realities
-   Barriers
-   The smallest meaningful next step

Compass should not generate a generic treatment menu.

## Risk Awareness

Compass should continuously monitor documented indicators related to
safety and other clinically significant concerns.

Risk information may need to interrupt the normal Room sequence when
immediate attention is clinically necessary.

Compass should highlight concerns and missing risk information while
preserving clinician responsibility for risk determination and action.

## Documentation Readiness

Compass should continuously evaluate whether sufficient evidence exists
to support required documentation.

This includes, where applicable:

-   Diagnosis
-   Functional impairment
-   Medical necessity
-   Level of care
-   Treatment direction
-   Regulatory or payer requirements
-   Golden Thread coherence

Much of this logic may remain **hidden but available** so the clinician
is not burdened with compliance information that is not immediately
needed.

Required note components should be generated automatically from
supported assessment evidence whenever possible.

# Compass Rooms

Compass Rooms are focused clinical reasoning workspaces.

They are **not separate datasets** and they are not simply renamed tabs.

Each Room applies a different clinical lens to the same evolving client
story.

> **One evolving clinical story → multiple purposeful clinical lenses →
> one integrated understanding and plan.**

Rooms define the clinical journey.

Intelligence functions support the work happening inside each Room.

Only one Room should be open at a time.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌊 Observation Room

### What are we seeing?

Purpose:

Stay close to the evidence and the client's story before deciding what
it means.

Primary content may include:

-   Live Clinical Overview
-   Executive Summary / Story So Far
-   Presenting concerns
-   Current symptoms
-   Functional impact
-   Relevant context
-   Emerging themes
-   Strengths and resources already visible
-   Missing information
-   "Compass Is Wondering..." prompts
-   Natural-language questions the clinician may ask next

Observation should describe before explaining.

It should **not** prematurely diagnose, formulate causation, or
recommend treatment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌿 Understanding Room

### How might this make sense?

Purpose:

Develop a compassionate, transparent formulation.

This Room should organize information around:

-   What happened to them?
-   How did they adapt, cope, protect themselves, or survive?
-   What patterns developed?
-   What biological, developmental, relational, psychological, social,
    medical, environmental, or systemic factors may help explain the
    current picture?
-   What strengths or protective processes developed alongside the
    difficulties?

Primary content may include:

-   Predisposing and precipitating factors
-   Adaptation and survival hypotheses
-   Attachment or relational patterns when supported
-   Trauma-related patterns when supported
-   Cognitive, emotional, behavioral, and physiological processes
-   Protective factors
-   Competing explanations
-   Supporting and missing evidence
-   Questions that could strengthen or weaken the formulation

Understanding should formulate without treating hypotheses as facts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧭 Navigation Room

### What matters most right now?

Purpose:

Help the clinician see the forest for the trees.

This Room should organize:

-   Maintaining factors
-   Safety and stabilization needs
-   Functional priorities
-   Client priorities
-   Diagnostic and differential questions that materially affect
    direction
-   Medical or psychiatric rule-outs
-   Readiness
-   Capacity
-   Environmental realities
-   Barriers
-   What needs attention now
-   What can wait
-   Clinical leverage points

Navigation should help the clinician identify the **route of least
unnecessary resistance** while preserving sound clinical judgment.

When the client has limited capacity, resources, safety, time, or
emotional bandwidth, Compass should help identify the smallest
clinically meaningful priority rather than producing an unrealistic
comprehensive plan.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌱 Growth Room

### What could help, and what is feasible now?

Purpose:

Translate understanding and priorities into realistic opportunities for
change.

Primary content may include:

-   Client-defined goals
-   Strengths and resources
-   Existing coping tools
-   Skills to strengthen
-   Evidence-informed treatment considerations
-   Psychoeducation
-   Behavioral experiments
-   Environmental or relational supports
-   Sequencing
-   Readiness
-   Barriers
-   Small next steps
-   Indicators of progress

Growth should emphasize **minimum effective change** when appropriate.

The best recommendation is not necessarily the most comprehensive
intervention. It is the clinically coherent intervention the person can
realistically use.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 📖 Reflection Room

### What have we learned, and how do we carry it forward?

Purpose:

Integrate the clinical picture and prepare conclusions, documentation,
and next steps.

Primary content may include:

-   Current formulation
-   Diagnostic summary and rationale
-   ICD-10 support
-   Evidence for and against diagnostic possibilities
-   Remaining uncertainty
-   Risk and protective factors
-   Golden Thread review
-   Documentation readiness
-   Documentation preview
-   Medical necessity and level-of-care support when needed
-   Items to monitor or revisit

Reflection should not hide uncertainty for the sake of a polished final
product.

Major conclusions should have been developed through earlier reasoning
rather than appearing for the first time here.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Room Boundaries

Room boundaries are clinically meaningful.

Compass should not allow every Room to do everything.

**Observation describes before interpreting.**

**Understanding formulates before prioritizing.**

**Navigation prioritizes before planning.**

**Growth translates formulation and priorities into feasible change.**

**Reflection integrates, evaluates, and documents.**

This sequencing reduces premature closure and keeps clinical guidance
aligned with the clinician's current reasoning task.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Real-Time Clinical Guidance

Clinical guidance should be woven organically throughout the assessment
and the Rooms rather than isolated inside a single "Coach" tab.

Compass may surface:

-   Questions to ask
-   Language the clinician could use
-   Areas to clarify
-   Patterns worth noticing
-   Competing hypotheses
-   Rapport considerations
-   Psychoeducation opportunities
-   Safety considerations
-   Diagnostic questions
-   Treatment considerations
-   Small feasible next steps

Guidance should be:

-   Brief
-   Timely
-   Specific
-   Evidence-linked
-   Clinically meaningful
-   Easy to ignore when not needed
-   Non-disruptive to rapport

The goal is for Compass to feel like an experienced supervisor sitting
quietly beside the clinician---not another task demanding attention.

# Visual and Workspace Behavior

Compass should feel alive, calm, and clinically grounded.

When meaningful clinical updates occur, the interface should communicate
those changes through subtle visual feedback that reinforces active
reasoning without distracting from the therapeutic interaction.

Possible behaviors include:

-   Gentle highlighting
-   Fade transitions
-   Soft pulsing
-   Confidence indicators updating
-   Newly appearing insights
-   Subtle state changes indicating meaningful updates

Visual feedback should communicate:

> "Compass noticed something important."

without interrupting the clinician's workflow.

## Assessment Mode and Room Mode

Compass should support two distinct workspace states.

### Assessment Mode

The clinician is gathering information with the client.

The assessment form and section navigation remain primary.

Compass may provide compact, real-time orientation and access to the
Compass Rooms.

### Room Mode

Selecting a Compass Room should feel like **entering a focused clinical
workspace**.

When a Room is opened:

-   The selected Room should become the primary workspace.
-   The ordinary assessment form and left-side assessment navigation may
    temporarily move out of view.
-   The Room should use substantially more screen space than the former
    side panel.
-   The interface should provide a clear route back to the assessment
    and/or Compass Rooms.
-   Only one Room should be open at a time.
-   The transition should feel like navigating to a purposeful
    destination, not expanding a card or opening an intrusive modal.
-   The Room remains connected to the live assessment data and should
    update as the assessment evolves.

A full-workspace transition is preferred over cramming deep clinical
reasoning into the narrow side panel.

## Room Lobby

When no Room is selected, Compass may present the five Rooms as a calm
navigation lobby:

-   Observation
-   Understanding
-   Navigation
-   Growth
-   Reflection

The lobby should communicate the clinical journey without forcing the
clinician to understand software architecture.

Experienced users may eventually receive faster room-switching controls,
but clarity and orientation take priority.

# User Experience Goals

The clinician should feel that Compass is:

-   Continuously thinking
-   Organizing information
-   Recognizing patterns
-   Holding complexity
-   Identifying missing information
-   Helping them see the forest for the trees
-   Suggesting meaningful next questions
-   Helping determine what matters now
-   Respecting the client's capacity and context
-   Helping maintain the big picture
-   Making documentation emerge from the clinical work already completed

The clinician should never feel that Compass is:

-   Attempting to take over the interview
-   Replacing professional judgment
-   Flooding them with recommendations
-   Asking them to navigate two competing systems
-   Turning every concern into a diagnosis
-   Treating every possible intervention as equally urgent
-   Adding compliance clutter to the clinical workspace

The interface should reduce cognitive load rather than relocating it.

# Success Criteria

A successful Intelligence Panel should leave clinicians saying:

-   "It helped me notice things I might have otherwise missed."
-   "It kept me focused on understanding the client."
-   "It asked the questions I didn't think to ask."
-   "It strengthened my clinical reasoning."
-   "It organized my thinking without taking over."

**---**

# Guiding Philosophy

Compass Intelligence is not a chatbot.

It is not a diagnosis engine.

It is not a documentation generator.

Compass is a proactive clinical reasoning system designed to help
clinicians develop the clearest possible understanding of the person
sitting in front of them.

Every recommendation should ultimately support one question:

> \*\*\*\*How can I better understand this person's story so I can
> provide the best possible care?\*\*\*\*

If a feature does not help clinicians answer that question, it does not
belong in Compass Intelligence.

## Current Implementation Direction --- August 14, 2026

The Room architecture is actively replacing the earlier permanent Story
/ Coach / Journey / Quality / Final tab model.

Current implementation direction:

-   `activeRoom = null` represents the Compass Rooms lobby.
-   Selecting a Room sets the active Room.
-   Only one Room should be open at a time.
-   `ObservationRoom.jsx` is the first dedicated Room component.
-   The Observation Room has been successfully rendered as its own
    workspace within the current panel architecture.
-   The legacy Story content has been conditionally removed while
    Observation is active.
-   The legacy tab strip has been conditionally hidden while Observation
    is active.
-   The next architectural step is to move active Rooms out of the
    narrow side panel and into a full-workspace Room Mode.
-   The assessment interface should return when the clinician exits the
    Room.

The existing implementation is a migration bridge, not the final Room
UX.
