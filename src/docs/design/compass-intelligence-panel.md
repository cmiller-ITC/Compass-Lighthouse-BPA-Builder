# Purpose

Behavioral health assessments require clinicians to gather, organize, interpret, and integrate an extraordinary amount of information within a limited period of time. During a typical 50–60 minute intake, clinicians must simultaneously establish rapport, obtain a comprehensive history, recognize diagnostic patterns, assess risk, determine appropriate treatment recommendations, and create documentation that accurately reflects the client's presentation.

This cognitive burden is substantial and increases the likelihood that meaningful clinical patterns, inconsistencies, or opportunities for deeper exploration may be overlooked.

The Compass Intelligence Panel was created to reduce this burden.

Rather than functioning as a documentation assistant or AI chatbot, Compass serves as a real-time clinical navigation and reasoning system that continuously helps clinicians organize, interpret, and refine their understanding of the person sitting in front of them.

Its purpose is not simply to help clinicians complete an assessment.

Its purpose is to help clinicians **understand the person as completely, accurately, and compassionately as possible within the time available**, allowing them to make the most informed diagnostic and treatment decisions possible.

Compass functions like an experienced clinical supervisor who actively thinks alongside the clinician throughout the interview—continuously observing, synthesizing, and proactively offering guidance while preserving complete clinician autonomy.

---

# Vision

The Compass Intelligence Panel is the clinician's real-time clinical thinking partner.

It continuously observes the evolving assessment, identifies meaningful clinical patterns, synthesizes information into an evolving understanding of the client's story, and proactively offers evidence-based guidance throughout the interview.

Rather than replacing clinical judgment, Compass exists to strengthen it.

The clinician remains the decision maker.

Compass simply helps them think more clearly.

---

# Clinical Mission

Throughout every assessment, Compass should continuously help the clinician answer six fundamental questions:

1. What story is emerging?

2. What clinical patterns am I seeing?

3. What evidence supports those observations?

4. What important information is still missing?

5. What should I explore next?

6. Based on everything currently known, what treatment direction appears most appropriate?

Every feature within the Intelligence Panel should directly contribute to answering one or more of these questions.

---

# Intelligence Cycle

Compass continuously operates using a five-stage clinical reasoning loop.

## 1. Observe

Continuously monitor all available assessment information including:

- Assessment responses
- Narrative text
- Symptom selections
- Standardized measures
- Mental Status Examination
- Timeline information
- Functional impairment
- Risk factors
- Psychiatric history
- Medical history
- Social history
- Clinician-entered observations

---

## 2. Interpret

Analyze incoming information to identify:

- Clinical themes
- Symptom patterns
- Functional impairments
- Protective factors
- Risk indicators
- Strengths
- Inconsistencies
- Missing information
- Diagnostic possibilities

---

## 3. Synthesize

Integrate all available information into an evolving understanding of the client's story.

Rather than viewing each response independently, Compass should continuously connect information across multiple assessment domains to develop a comprehensive clinical formulation.

---

## 4. Guide

Provide proactive clinical guidance that helps the clinician think more effectively.

Examples include:

- Executive summaries
- Clinical snapshots
- Emerging themes
- Clarifying questions
- Diagnostic considerations
- ICD-10 diagnostic suggestions
- Treatment implications
- Risk awareness
- Documentation guidance

---

## 5. Update

Immediately reflect meaningful changes as new information becomes available.

The Intelligence Panel should feel alive, continuously adapting its understanding of the case as the interview progresses.

# Core Design Principles

## Clinical First

Every recommendation must originate from documented clinical evidence.

Compass should never invent symptoms, fabricate history, or make unsupported assumptions.

---

## Understand Before Diagnosing

The primary objective is understanding—not assigning diagnoses.

Diagnosis is the result of thoughtful clinical reasoning rather than the starting point.

---

## Proactive Guidance

Compass is intentionally proactive.

It should never wait for the clinician to request assistance.

Instead, it continuously evaluates the assessment and independently offers guidance whenever meaningful clinical changes occur.

Examples include:

- Newly emerging themes
- Diagnostic confidence changes
- Missing information
- Contradictory evidence
- Recommended follow-up questions
- Treatment implications
- Documentation gaps

Guidance should always feel timely, relevant, and supportive without interrupting the therapeutic interaction.

---

## Transparent Clinical Reasoning

Compass should never function as a black box.

Every recommendation should answer:

- What am I seeing?
- Why do I think this?
- What evidence supports it?
- What evidence is still missing?
- How confident am I?

---

## Dynamic

Compass is never static.

Every response entered during the assessment has the potential to strengthen, weaken, refine, or redirect the evolving clinical understanding.

---

## Assist, Never Replace

Compass recommends.

Clinicians decide.

The clinician always maintains authority over diagnostic impressions, treatment decisions, and documentation.

# Intelligence Outputs

The Intelligence Panel continuously generates clinically meaningful guidance.

## Executive Summary

A real-time bird's-eye view of the case.

This section should answer:

> "If another clinician walked into the room right now, what would they need to know within 30 seconds?"

---

## Clinical Snapshot

A concise clinical narrative summarizing the client's presentation, major concerns, and current formulation.

---

## What I'm Seeing

Observed clinical themes and meaningful patterns.

Examples include:

- Chronic autonomic hyperarousal
- Perfectionism
- Avoidance
- Emotional suppression
- Executive dysfunction
- Attachment insecurity
- Emotional dysregulation

These are observations—not diagnoses.

---

## What Doesn't Yet Add Up

Identify inconsistencies, unanswered questions, or areas requiring clarification.

The purpose is curiosity rather than contradiction.

---

## Clarifying Questions

Generate thoughtful follow-up questions that reduce diagnostic uncertainty and deepen understanding.

Questions should help distinguish competing diagnoses, clarify severity, explore maintaining factors, and uncover missing history.

---

## Differential Diagnosis

Continuously estimate diagnostic possibilities based on documented evidence.

Each diagnostic consideration should include:

- Supporting evidence
- Missing evidence
- Confidence level
- Recommended clarification questions

---

## Clinical Themes

Extract recurring themes that may guide treatment planning.

Examples include:

- Safety
- Control
- Shame
- Identity
- Avoidance
- Perfectionism
- Emotional inhibition
- People pleasing
- Attachment

---

## Treatment Implications

Suggest evidence-supported treatment approaches based upon the emerging formulation.

Examples may include:

- CBT
- ACT
- ERP
- DBT
- Pain Reprocessing Therapy
- IFS
- Attachment-focused interventions
- Behavioral Activation
- Motivational Interviewing

Recommendations should always explain why they fit the current formulation.

---

## Risk Awareness

Continuously monitor documented indicators related to:

- Suicide risk
- Homicide risk
- Psychosis
- Mania
- Abuse
- Neglect
- Self-neglect
- Medical concerns

Compass should highlight concerns but never independently determine risk.

---

## Documentation Readiness

Continuously evaluate whether sufficient evidence exists to support:

- Diagnosis
- Medical necessity
- Functional impairment
- Level of care
- Treatment recommendations
- Insurance documentation

---

# Visual Behavior

Compass should feel alive.

When meaningful clinical updates occur, the interface should communicate those changes through subtle visual feedback that reinforces active reasoning without distracting from the therapeutic interaction.

Possible behaviors include:

- Gentle highlighting
- Fade transitions
- Soft pulsing
- Confidence indicators updating
- Newly appearing insights
- Color changes indicating meaningful updates

Visual feedback should communicate:

> "Compass noticed something important."

without interrupting the clinician's workflow.

---

# User Experience Goals

The clinician should feel that Compass is:

- Continuously thinking
- Organizing information
- Recognizing patterns
- Identifying missing information
- Suggesting meaningful next steps
- Helping maintain the "big picture"

The clinician should never feel that Compass is attempting to take over the interview or replace professional judgment.

---

# Success Criteria

A successful Intelligence Panel should leave clinicians saying:

- "It helped me notice things I might have otherwise missed."
- "It kept me focused on understanding the client."
- "It asked the questions I didn't think to ask."
- "It strengthened my clinical reasoning."
- "It organized my thinking without taking over."

---

# Guiding Philosophy

Compass Intelligence is not a chatbot.

It is not a diagnosis engine.

It is not a documentation generator.

Compass is a proactive clinical reasoning system designed to help clinicians develop the clearest possible understanding of the person sitting in front of them.

Every recommendation should ultimately support one question:

> **How can I better understand this person's story so I can provide the best possible care?**

If a feature does not help clinicians answer that question, it does not belong in Compass Intelligence.
