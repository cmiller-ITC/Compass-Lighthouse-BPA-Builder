# Compass Biopsychosocial Assessment (BPA) Clinical Flow
**Version:** 1.0 (Working Draft)  
**Status:** Clinical Architecture  
**Author:** Carrie Miller, EdD, LCSW-C

---

# Purpose

The Compass Biopsychosocial Assessment (BPA) is designed to guide clinicians through a comprehensive, clinically meaningful interview while simultaneously supporting real-time clinical reasoning.

Unlike traditional electronic health records that simply collect information, Compass is intended to function as an active clinical thinking partner.

Throughout the assessment, Compass continuously organizes information, identifies emerging patterns, highlights missing information, suggests areas requiring further exploration, and prepares the clinician for diagnostic formulation, treatment planning, and documentation.

The interview should feel natural, conversational, and patient-centered—not like completing a checklist.

---

# Guiding Philosophy

Every biopsychosocial assessment should progressively answer four fundamental clinical questions.

---

# 1. What is happening?

Understand the client's current experience.

This portion of the interview establishes:

- Why the client is seeking care now
- Presenting concerns
- Current symptoms
- Severity
- Duration
- Frequency
- Functional impairment
- Immediate safety concerns
- Current distress

Primary Clinical Goal:

> Develop an accurate picture of the client's current clinical presentation before attempting to explain why it exists.

---

# 2. How did we get here?

Understand the factors contributing to the client's current presentation.

This includes:

- Developmental history
- Family dynamics
- Attachment experiences
- Trauma history
- Psychiatric history
- Medical history
- Substance use
- Social history
- Protective factors
- Maintaining factors
- Historical patterns

Primary Clinical Goal:

> Identify the biological, psychological, developmental, relational, and environmental factors contributing to the current presentation.

---

# 3. What needs to be different?

Understand what meaningful improvement looks like from the client's perspective.

This section explores:

- Client goals
- Values
- Motivation
- Readiness
- Desired outcomes
- Quality of life
- Recovery vision

Primary Clinical Goal:

> Ensure treatment is aligned with what matters most to the client.

---

# 4. What resources do we have to get there?

Develop a realistic, achievable treatment approach.

Explore:

- Internal strengths
- Existing coping skills
- Family supports
- Community supports
- Financial considerations
- Insurance limitations
- Transportation
- Time availability
- Medical providers
- Current resources
- Barriers to care

Primary Clinical Goal:

> Build a treatment plan that is both clinically appropriate and realistically achievable.

---

# Clinical Interview Principles

Compass should guide clinicians to:

• Stay curious before concluding

• Observe before interpreting

• Understand before diagnosing

• Collaborate before directing

• Match recommendations to the client's actual circumstances

The assessment should feel like a conversation rather than an interrogation.

Questions should build naturally from the client's story.

Compass should only recommend additional questions when doing so would meaningfully improve diagnostic confidence or treatment planning.

---

# Clinical Thinking Sequence

The Compass Biopsychosocial Assessment should follow the natural progression of a therapeutic conversation rather than the structure of a traditional intake form.

The clinician should first develop a thorough understanding of the client's current experience before exploring historical contributors or developing treatment recommendations.

Clinical reasoning should naturally progress through the following stages.

---

## Stage 1 — Why Now?

Purpose:

Understand why the client is seeking treatment at this particular moment.

Questions Compass Helps Answer:

- What prompted treatment now?
- What has changed recently?
- What feels most difficult today?
- What concerns the client the most?
- Why now instead of three months ago?

Primary Clinical Goal:

Establish the context for the current episode before exploring symptoms.

---

## Stage 2 — Characterize the Current Experience

Purpose:

Develop a detailed understanding of each symptom the client identifies.

Rather than asking broad diagnostic questions, Compass encourages clinicians to understand the lived experience of each symptom.

For every endorsed symptom domain, explore:

- What does this look like?
- When did it begin?
- How often does it occur?
- How long does it last?
- How severe is it?
- What precipitates it?
- What makes it worse?
- What makes it better?
- How difficult is it to interrupt?
- How does it affect daily functioning?

Clinical Goal:

Develop sufficient understanding of the symptom before moving on.

---

## Stage 3 — Functional Impact

Purpose:

Understand how symptoms interfere with everyday life.

Areas include:

- Employment
- School
- Relationships
- Parenting
- Sleep
- Self-care
- Executive functioning
- Daily routines
- Physical health
- Recreation
- Social engagement

Clinical Goal:

Determine the degree of impairment and identify areas requiring intervention.

---

## Stage 4 — Does This Seem Situational or Longstanding?

Purpose:

Determine whether the current presentation reflects:

- A recent situational response
- A recurrence of previous symptoms
- A longstanding pattern
- A developmental pattern
- Multiple overlapping conditions

Clinical Goal:

Decide whether the interview should remain focused on current circumstances or transition into deeper historical exploration.

---

## Stage 5 — Rewind: Understanding the Story

Purpose:

Once the current presentation is understood, explore how the client became the person they are today.

Areas include:

- Childhood experiences
- Family relationships
- Attachment
- Trauma
- Developmental experiences
- Psychiatric history
- Medical history
- Substance use
- Cultural influences
- Significant life events

Clinical Focus:

> What happened?

> How did the client adapt?

> Which adaptations continue to influence current functioning?

---

## Stage 6 — What Keeps It Going?

Purpose:

Identify the factors maintaining the client's current difficulties.

Examples:

- Avoidance
- Rumination
- Hypervigilance
- Sleep disruption
- Relationship dynamics
- Chronic pain
- Financial stress
- Isolation
- Substance use
- Environmental instability
- Limited coping skills

Clinical Goal:

Identify perpetuating factors that should become treatment targets.

---

## Stage 7 — What Needs To Be Different?

Purpose:

Understand meaningful change from the client's perspective.

Examples:

- Reduce anxiety
- Improve sleep
- Improve relationships
- Better emotional regulation
- Feel happier
- Return to work
- Improve communication
- Increase confidence

Clinical Goal:

Develop collaborative treatment goals.

---

## Stage 8 — What Strengths & Resources Can Help?

Purpose:

Determine what the client already possesses that may support recovery.

Internal Resources:

- Insight
- Motivation
- Coping skills
- Resilience
- Values
- Readiness

External Resources:

- Family
- Friends
- Community
- Transportation
- Insurance
- Finances
- Medical providers
- Housing
- Time availability

Clinical Goal:

Ensure treatment recommendations are realistic and achievable.

---

## Stage 9 — Build the Path Forward

Purpose:

Integrate everything learned throughout the interview.

Compass synthesizes:

- Current presentation
- Historical contributors
- Adaptive patterns
- Perpetuating factors
- Client goals
- Strengths
- Available resources

Clinical Goal:

Develop a clinically appropriate and feasible treatment plan.

---
# Clinical Sufficiency Criteria

Compass should not determine that a symptom domain has been adequately assessed simply because required fields have been completed.

Instead, Compass evaluates whether sufficient clinical understanding has been achieved.

For each endorsed symptom domain, clinicians should generally understand:

- Precipitating factors
- Symptom characteristics
- Duration
- Frequency
- Severity
- Triggers
- Exacerbating factors
- Relieving factors
- Functional impact
- Associated symptoms
- Relevant risk considerations

Once sufficient information has been gathered, Compass may indicate that the symptom has been adequately characterized.

---

## Clinical Sufficiency ≠ Form Completion

Traditional documentation asks:

> Have all required fields been completed?

Compass asks:

> Do we genuinely understand this person's experience well enough to move forward?
The purpose of Compass is to support clinical reasoning rather than checklist completion.

---

# Compass Intelligence

As information is collected, Compass continuously updates five clinical reasoning workspaces.

These workspaces mature alongside the interview rather than waiting until the assessment is complete.

---

# Observation Room

Primary Question:

**What are we seeing?**

Purpose:

Capture observations without making premature conclusions.

Focus Areas:

- Presenting concerns
- Symptoms
- Functional impairment
- Mental status observations
- Risk indicators
- Protective factors
- Emerging themes
- Missing information

Observation Room should never attempt diagnosis.

---

# Understanding Room

Primary Question:

**What connects?**

Purpose:

Identify relationships between information.

Examples:

- Trauma → hypervigilance
- ADHD → executive dysfunction
- Chronic pain → depression
- Sleep disruption → emotional dysregulation
- Financial stress → treatment barriers

The goal is pattern recognition rather than diagnosis.

---

# Navigation Room

Primary Question:

**What matters next?**

Purpose:

Guide the clinician toward the most useful next question.

Examples:

- Clarify onset
- Explore trauma history
- Assess functional impairment
- Evaluate safety
- Clarify strengths
- Assess protective factors

Navigation should reduce cognitive load during interviews.

---

# Growth Room

Primary Question:

**Where do we go from here?**

Purpose:

Translate understanding into treatment.

Examples:

- Initial treatment goals
- Recommended interventions
- Psychoeducation opportunities
- Therapeutic modalities
- Community resources
- Referrals
- Homework ideas

Growth recommendations must always consider feasibility.

---

# Reflection Room

Primary Question:

**Are we ready to conclude?**

Purpose:

Prepare for documentation and clinical decision making.

Includes:

- Executive Summary
- Diagnostic considerations
- Missing information
- Documentation quality
- Confidence level
- Treatment recommendations
- Narrative generation readiness

Reflection determines whether sufficient evidence exists before documentation is finalized.

---

# Design Principle

The BPA is not a form.

It is a structured clinical conversation.

Compass exists to support clinical judgment—not replace it.

Every question should serve a meaningful clinical purpose.

Every recommendation should be explainable.

Every conclusion should be supported by evidence gathered during the interview.

## Room Responsibilities

### Observation Room

Answers:

> **What are we seeing?**

Responsible for:

- Capturing facts without interpretation
- Highlighting symptoms
- Tracking functional impairment
- Monitoring safety
- Identifying strengths
- Detecting missing information

---

### Understanding Room

Answers:

> **What connects?**

Responsible for:

- Recognizing patterns
- Linking symptoms
- Connecting history to present functioning
- Identifying possible perpetuating factors
- Generating clinical hypotheses

---

### Navigation Room

Answers:

> **What matters next?**

Responsible for:

- Recommending follow-up questions
- Prioritizing areas requiring clarification
- Identifying missing evidence
- Suggesting additional assessment
- Reducing clinician cognitive load

---

### Growth Room

Answers:

> **Where do we go from here?**

Responsible for:

- Building treatment goals
- Recommending interventions
- Identifying strengths to leverage
- Suggesting psychoeducation
- Matching recommendations to client capacity and available resources

### Reflection Room

Answers:

> **Are we ready to conclude?**

Responsible for:

- Executive Summary
- Clinical Impression
- Diagnostic confidence
- Missing documentation
- Narrative generation
- Treatment plan readiness
- Quality review

# The Rewind Principle

Compass first seeks to understand the client's current experience before exploring historical contributors.

Once the clinician has developed a clear understanding of the present, Compass helps determine whether the presentation appears primarily situational, recurrent, longstanding, developmental, trauma-related, or multifactorial.

If deeper exploration is warranted, Compass guides the clinician to "rewind" the client's story.

Rather than asking about childhood because it is a required section of the assessment, Compass asks because understanding the client's history helps explain how current patterns developed and what adaptive strategies may continue to influence functioning today.
